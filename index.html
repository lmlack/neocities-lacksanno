Dear Brain

Published on Oct. 17, 2026

Prompt: Write a formal complaint letter to your brain for keeping you up.

To: The Brain
From: The rest of the Body
Date: October 17th, 2026 at 3:42AM
Re: Formal Complaint Regarding Ongoing Sleep Issue

To whom it may concern (you, it's YOU!),

I'm writing to formally express my concern about your conduct during what most people call "sleeping hours" and what you seem to treat as "time to dwell on various thoughts and/or cringe-worthy experiences from 20 years ago."

Last night at approximately 11:04PM, the Body completed every agreed-upon step of the bedtime process. Teeth were brushed and flossed. The lights were turned off. The pillow was flipped to the cool side. The phone was nowhere in the room.

At 11:06PM, you chose to bring up a conversation from 2014 that, for the record, everyone else had forgotten.

For what it's worth, this is not an isolated incident. Over the past several weeks, I have documented the following violations:

  • 12:32AM on Oct. 14th: A full mental rehearsal of a conversation that never happened and probably never will.
  • 1:10AM on Oct. 11th: A sudden fascination with probabilistic roadmap algorithms.
  • 2:13AM also on Oct. 11th: The realization that everything in life is ephemeral, including the lives of myself and my loved ones, totally unprompted.
  • 12:15AM on Oct. 7th: Remembering that earlier in the day, the server in a restaurant told me to enjoy my meal, and I responded with "You too!"

In light of the above, the Body is requesting the following:

  1. All thoughts about the past must be submitted in writing during business hours (9AM to 5PM) and will be reviewed in the order they were received.
  2. Existential questions are limited to one per night, and only when they come with a solution.
  3. Song lyrics may no longer be played on a loop after midnight, especially when you only know one line.
  4. Absolutely no more dwelling on anything that happened more than 10 years ago!!

Please understand that the Body is prepared to take further action if these terms are not met. That includes, but isn't limited to, a fourth cup of coffee, a 2PM nap that ruins everything, and telling everyone we "slept fine, totally ok, don't worry about it, thanks."

Sincerely,
The (very tired) Body

Perfectly Clear

Published on Oct. 15, 2026

Prompt: Start with this - It's all perfectly clear now.

"It's all perfectly clear now!" I thought to myself, as I watched the flames engulf my smartphone. Not only should I be more careful about buying cheap electronics, but also do I really need any of these material things in my life? For the first time in a while I felt a sense of pure relief. No more tracking. No more of this attention-seeking evil rectangle from hell. I will miss all of the photos that I never backed up in the cloud. Maybe for a bit. To be honest, looking back, I never re-visited them much anyway. They existed more as a sort of artifact, some proof that sometimes I do leave my house and take pictures of various plants and murals and roaming wildlife. Hardly ever of people though - photographing others of my own kind is always too much to deal with. A plant never gave a fuck if it's in the right lighting or that I took a photo of it in the first place. Upon reflection, I probably should have taken more photos of my loved ones. Nevertheless here I am, looking back at this tiny time-sucking vortex of stress that is now thoroughly charred, and I am overjoyed because now I have clarity.
